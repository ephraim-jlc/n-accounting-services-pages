What Is VAT and How Does It Work in the UK?

VAT (Value Added Tax) is a tax applied to many goods and services. VAT-registered businesses usually charge VAT on eligible sales and may be able to reclaim VAT on eligible business purchases.

The difference between VAT charged on sales and VAT reclaimed on purchases is reported to HMRC through a VAT return. For many businesses this is a regular reporting responsibility requiring accurate records, clear processes and timely submissions.

Good VAT support helps make sure sales and purchases are treated correctly, records are organised and returns are prepared using reliable information — especially important for businesses working towards Making Tax Digital.

Different VAT schemes affect how VAT is calculated, recorded and paid. The right approach can make VAT easier to manage and may improve cash flow depending on your business. Advice should always be based on your circumstances, taking into account aspects such as turnover, sector, customer type, expenses and cash flow.

FAQ · accordion + FAQ schema

Frequently Asked Questions

What is VAT in the UK? –
VAT stands for Value Added Tax — a tax applied to many goods and services. VAT-registered businesses usually charge VAT on eligible sales and may reclaim VAT on eligible business purchases.
How do VAT returns work? +
A VAT return reports VAT charged on sales and VAT paid on purchases over a set reporting period. It's submitted to HMRC and shows whether VAT is payable or reclaimable.
Do I need to register for VAT? +
You may need to register if your taxable turnover reaches the current VAT registration threshold. Some businesses also register voluntarily. An accountant can help you understand whether registration is required or beneficial.
What is the VAT threshold in the UK? +
The VAT threshold is the level of taxable turnover at which a business must register for VAT. This figure can change, so it should always be checked against current HMRC guidance before making a decision.
What is the Flat Rate Scheme? +
A VAT scheme designed to simplify VAT calculations for some small businesses. Instead of reclaiming VAT on most purchases, businesses pay a fixed percentage of turnover based on their sector. Suitability depends on the business and should be reviewed carefully.
