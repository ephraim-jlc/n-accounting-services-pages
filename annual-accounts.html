What Are Annual Accounts?

Annual accounts are financial reports prepared at the end of a business’s financial year. For limited companies, statutory accounts are a legal requirement and must be prepared in line with UK reporting rules.

They summarise the financial position and performance of the business (e.g., income, costs, assets, liabilities and profit), and may be used for Companies House filing, HMRC submissions, Corporation Tax calculations, funding applications and general business review.

Accurate accounts help show your business is compliant, financially transparent and properly managed. Poorly prepared or late accounts can lead to penalties, compliance issues and reduced confidence from lenders, suppliers or investors.

Annual Accounts Are Necessary For:

  • Maintaining accurate financial records
  • Demonstrating business credibility
  • Understanding business performance
  • Meeting Companies House requirements
  • Supporting HMRC submissions
  • Preparing Corporation Tax information
  • Supporting funding or mortgage applications

Companies House Filing Requirements and Deadlines

Limited companies are required to file annual accounts with Companies House. These must be submitted by the relevant deadline and must be accurate, complete and suitable for the company’s reporting requirements.

Missing a filing deadline can result in penalties and may affect how your business is viewed by lenders, suppliers and other stakeholders. Late or inaccurate filings also create unnecessary stress and admin for directors.

N-Accounting helps businesses prepare and file annual accounts correctly and on time — managing the process, checking the required information and supporting the right filing approach for your company.

HMRC Submission and Corporation Tax Alignment

Annual accounts are not only important for Companies House. They also provide the basis for calculating taxable profits and preparing Corporation Tax information for HMRC.

For limited companies, year-end accounts and Corporation Tax are closely connected. If the accounts are inaccurate or incomplete, the tax calculation may also be wrong, leading to compliance issues, unexpected liabilities or delays.

We help ensure your annual accounts, Corporation Tax return information and HMRC submissions are properly aligned, giving you greater confidence that year-end reporting has been handled accurately.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are annual accounts? –
Financial reports prepared at the end of a business’s financial year, summarising income, costs, profit, assets, liabilities and the overall financial position.
When are annual accounts due in the UK? +
Deadlines depend on the business type and reporting period. Limited companies must file with Companies House by the relevant deadline. An accountant can help confirm your correct filing date.
Do I need an accountant for annual accounts? +
Not always legally, but many businesses choose to because accounts must be accurate and compliant. An accountant reduces errors, saves time and helps ensure the correct information is filed.
What happens if I file accounts late? +
Late filing can lead to penalties and may affect your company’s public record, as well as creating issues with lenders, suppliers or others who review your business information.
What is included in statutory accounts? +
Usually a profit and loss account, balance sheet and supporting notes, depending on the type and size of the company.
